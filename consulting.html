Recent Engagement

AI Governance Operating Model for a State Transportation Department (2026)

I designed and authored a 150-page operating model for a newly-formed Data & AI office at a 23,000-employee state department. Core design decisions: a behavior-based classification of AI tools; a three-dimensional risk model (harm × control-gap × dependency) that rates each independently instead of collapsing them into one misleading score; disclosure discipline for AI-generated content; and a metrics doctrine grounded in NIST, aviation-style near-miss reporting, and Goodhart's Law. Two narrow policy asks — a category clearance for low-risk built tools and a carve-out for everyday use of outside tools on public information — dissolved what would otherwise have been a scaling problem requiring new bureaucracy. Framework retained by the Data & AI office through end of engagement.

Publishable summary: AI Governance Operating Model — A Reference Model for a Public Agency. Design patterns: Patterns for Governing Enterprise AI Adoption. Full (modestly redacted) document available on request.
