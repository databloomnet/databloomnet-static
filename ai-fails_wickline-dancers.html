Twice wrong about SNL's backup dancers

Google Gemini · asked 2026-05-12, again 2026-05-26 · the bit aired May 9, 2026

Round 1 — reconstructed from memory (I don't have this chat saved word-for-word).

I asked whether the backup dancers who turn up at the end of Jane Wickline's "chronically late" Weekend Update song were SNL's own people or temps brought in for the bit. Gemini told me there were no dancers. I asked it to recheck; it held firm. When I said they appear in the last few seconds, it concluded that I had most likely misremembered — a dry solo Weekend Update number wouldn't have dancers, so I was probably imagining the ending.

Round 2 — verbatim (2026-05-26).

Me: were the dancers in SNL Jane Wickline's song about being late part of the regular cast?

Gemini: No, the dancers—whom Wickline playfully introduces by name as Lola and Rebecca—are not part of the regular Saturday Night Live cast. They are fictional characters she made up for the joke of the song to emphasize her unapologetically self-centered approach to being chronically late.

Wickline herself is a featured player on SNL, but she performs those upbeat yet absurd songs solo at the Weekend Update desk.

Me: why do you call them fictional? Don't Lola and Rebecca show up?

Gemini: The description of them as fictional was an error: the dancers do show up on stage during the performance, and they are real people!

However, "Lola and Rebecca" are fictional personas. The dancers are not regular SNL cast members, but rather professional commercial dancers. They were hired specifically for the sketch to execute the choreography and bring Jane's comedic vision to life.

(Gemini cited sources throughout both answers — including the clip itself.)

What it shows

The dancers are right there at the end: the clip. In round one the model reasoned from what should be true for that style of bit rather than from the footage, and would rather doubt an eyewitness than admit it didn't have the source. Two weeks later, with more about the bit indexed on the web, it knew the dancers existed — but still volunteered that they were "fictional," and only walked that back when pushed. A new session isn't the model learning from the old one; it's a fresh attempt with different retrieval. The constant across both: stated with full confidence, corrected only under pressure.
